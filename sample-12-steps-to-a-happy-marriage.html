12 Steps to a Happy Marriage

A sample from the Introduction.

Introduction

Let me start off by saying this book is not written by a marriage counselor, a therapist, or anyone with a degree in psychology. Nope. This book is written by a guy who burps too loud, forgets to pick up his socks, and occasionally weaponizes a fart with the help of a house fan.

So, if you’re looking for charts, statistics, or twelve scientifically proven steps to “rekindle the flame,” you might want to put this book down right now and head to the self-help section. But if you want real stories, the kind that actually happen in a marriage, farts that rock the house, the thermostat wars, the petty arguments about dishes and laundry, then welcome. You’re in the right place.

Because marriage, in my experience, isn’t about the big, dramatic moments. Sure, the wedding day is important, the anniversaries are nice, and the vacations are memorable. But what really makes or breaks a marriage are the tiny, ridiculous, everyday things.

It’s the burp that makes your spouse wrinkle her nose. It’s the silent fart that gets blasted across the living room by your fan.

It’s the thermostat that somehow becomes a matter of life and death.

It’s discovering that the person you love most in the world has fifty-eight bottles of hair products, none of which you can identify.

Those are the real battles of marriage. And let me tell you something: there are no victories. None. If you think you’re winning in marriage, you’re probably just not paying attention. Marriage isn’t about winning, it’s about compromise, survival, and the occasional truce that keeps you both from losing your minds. And quite possibly, ending up in jail.

Now, I didn’t grow up thinking this way. When I was a kid, I thought marriage was something adults just did. My mom and dad had one bathroom, three boys, and a whole lot of chaos. My mom, bless her soul, somehow survived that environment, and looking back, I realize I inherited some habits from that upbringing that weren’t exactly “wife approved.” Dirty clothes on the floor, dishes left wherever I finished eating, water spilled and kicked aside. To me, those things were normal. To my wife, they were signs of the end times.

So, when I moved in with her, I got an education in “Maintaining Your Home 101.” I learned about hampers, about wiping down shower doors, about the proper way to load a dishwasher (apparently there is a right way), and about the catastrophic damage a single ice cube can do to a hardwood floor.

This book is my collection of those lessons. Not written as rules, but as stories. Because stories stick. Stories make us laugh. Stories remind us that marriage isn’t just hard work, it’s also comedy, tragedy, and sometimes straight-up nonsense.

Here’s how it works:

Each chapter starts with the Tall Tale version of what happened, the exaggerated, over-the-top disaster version where a burp leads to divorce papers or a pile of laundry nearly ends the relationship. Then I’ll pull it back and give you the Real Story, which is usually far less dramatic, but still enough to cause a fight, an eye roll, or at least a sigh. Finally, I’ll wrap it up with the Lesson, not a lecture, not advice from an expert, but just the truth I learned from that moment. And here’s the kicker: after twenty-three years together (sixteen of them married), I still don’t have it all figured out.

I still forget, still mess up, still annoy her, and still get annoyed myself. But we’re here. We’ve survived the burps, the farts, the thermostats, and the chips in the bed. And if we can survive those things, maybe you can too. So, this isn’t 12 Steps to a Perfect Marriage. It’s more like 12 Stories About How Not to Kill Each Other and Maybe Even Laugh While You’re at It. If that sounds like the kind of marriage book you can handle, then turn the page. Just don’t do it with greasy fingers, you’ll leave smudges, and trust me, that never ends well.
