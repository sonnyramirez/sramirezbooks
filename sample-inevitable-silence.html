Inevitable Silence

The opening of Chapter 1, Across The Street.

Chapter 1: Across The Street

Archie noticed the van before it reached the corner. There was nothing special about the van. What stood out was the way the driver eased it into the parking space without ever tapping the brakes or revving the engine once.

Archie stood in the shadows of his kitchen, watching the moving van, for no other reason than having a feeling in his gut that he had learned long ago to trust. The van shut off and two movers hopped out of the passenger side, walked to the back and unlocked the doors. They started unloading, and they did it faster and neater than any movers Archie had ever seen.

Archie, however, wasn’t watching them. He was watching the driver. The cat stepped out last. He had white fur with a noticeable light brown mark over the left side of his face. He definitely wasn’t the fancy type, but he was put together. He wore a dark coat cut too well to be accidental and shoes that had seen wear but no neglect.

When he got out of the van, he didn’t stretch or yawn. He just stood there with his paws together while his eyes moved over the street in a slow, deliberate arc. To Archie it looked like he was counting exits and remembering cars parked in front of homes. Archie felt the fur on the back of his neck stand up. The cat’s gaze passed over Archie’s house and lingered for a second longer than necessary. Then he nodded once to himself and started unloading boxes as well.

Archie stepped back from the window. He knew that cat from somewhere, and it took him a minute before it came back to him. It was the cat from Hawaii. “Why was he here, across the street from me?” Archie said to himself.

Archie lay low for a day, and then on a Sunday morning he left out the back door, crossed through his neighbor’s yard behind him, and walked the long way to the neighborhood grocery store. Once inside, he looked around and grabbed some milk and bread, then went to the counter to talk to the clerk.

“Hey Archie, how you been?” asked the clerk.

“Been all right. Anything new and exciting going on around here?” Archie said to the clerk.

“Nah, not really…, wait a minute, Oh yeah, there’s this new cat living around here a British fellow very polite well spoken. Doesn’t like to be called Eric though. I once made that mistake when the receipt popped out and had his name on it. I remember it had the name Mister Eric Randall on it, so I thought his name was Mr. Eric Randall not Mister Eric Randall you know what I mean? Anyways, he said, very politely, my name’s Mister Eric not Eric, or something like that I wasn’t paying that much attention. Anyways, I said your first name’s Eric or Mister? He goes Mister. Strange first name wouldn’t you say?”

Archie almost regretted asking this clerk because he could go on and on. When he finished talking, Archie thanked him and left.

The name stuck with Archie the whole walk home.

This wasn’t a coincidence. He must’ve planned this out. But for what?

They crossed paths a couple of days later, early in the morning, when the fog still sat low on the street and every little sound carried. Archie locked his door and turned at the exact moment Mister Eric stepped out across the street. They both stopped without meaning to.

Up close, Archie could see more. There was a scar along Mister Eric’s knuckle, and the coat couldn’t hide the way he carried himself. He was military, no question about it, and he didn’t look like a cat who needed to prove anything to anyone on this block. Archie didn’t smile.

“What are you doing here? I remember you. You’re the cat from Hawaii,” Archie said.
